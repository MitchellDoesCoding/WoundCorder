For Clinicians (Nursing / Non–Wound-Specialist Section)
This section is written for clinical staff, not patients. It summarizes safe interim management until a wound-care or other specialist can be involved.

General principles (apply to every wound)
Use the TIME / TIMERS wound-bed-preparation framework as the organizing approach for any chronic or non-healing wound (Alam et al., Journal of the American Geriatrics Society, 2021; Burkhart et al., VA/DoD Pressure Injury Guideline, 2026):

T – Tissue: Remove nonviable tissue. Cleanse all wounds to loosen debris with potable water or normal saline; no cleanser is clearly superior, but use an antimicrobial cleanser (e.g., hypochlorous acid, dilute povidone-iodine) if bacterial burden is rising. Avoid wet-to-dry dressings — they desiccate the bed, debride indiscriminately, and are painful. Do NOT debride dry, ischemic, or eschar-covered lower-extremity wounds until a vascular assessment is done.
I – Infection / inflammation: Watch for covert infection (friable hypergranulation, increasing exudate, odor, delayed healing, epithelial bridging). Topical antimicrobial dressings (silver, iodine, medical-grade honey, gentian violet) may help short-term; avoid prolonged use (cytotoxicity). Spreading cellulitis, systemic signs, or deep infection require systemic antibiotics and prompt escalation.
M – Moisture balance: Match the dressing to exudate. Dry wounds → hydrogel or hydrocolloid to donate moisture; exudative wounds → alginate, hydrofiber, or foam to absorb. Protect peri-wound skin with a barrier/skin protectant to prevent maceration.
E – Edge: Protect and support the advancing epithelial edge; maceration or rolled (epibole) edges stall healing.
R / S – Repair & social factors: address nutrition, glycemic control, pain, adherence, and patient education.
Baseline documentation for any wound: location, size, depth, exudate, tissue type, peri-wound skin, pain, and odor; check tetanus status for any acute/traumatic wound and vaccinate if the last booster was >10 years ago (or >5 years for dirty wounds).

When to escalate to a specialist
Any wound failing to improve after ~4 weeks of appropriate care, or not healed by ~12 weeks.
Suspected arterial insufficiency/ischemia, osteomyelitis, deep or spreading infection, or systemic sepsis.
Burns or wounds involving the face, joints, bone, tendons, nerves, or large body-surface area.
Atypical or non-healing wounds that may need biopsy to exclude malignancy or other etiology.
